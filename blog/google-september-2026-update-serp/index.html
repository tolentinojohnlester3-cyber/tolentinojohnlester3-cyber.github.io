Google’s September 2026 update in the SERP

On 24 September 2026 Google started a global spam update. The results page does not flip in one hour. Organic positions can keep moving for up to two weeks. Here is what the SERP is doing, what Google confirmed, and how to read a drop while the rollout is still live.

Key takeaway

The September 2026 release is a spam update, still rolling out as of 29 September. It started at 09:15 PDT on 24 September, covers every language, and can take up to two weeks. Organic rankings move in stages. Google did not announce a new SERP feature.

What Google confirmed

Google posted the September 2026 spam update on the Search Status Dashboard on 24 September 2026. Search Engine Journal logged the start at 09:15 a.m. PDT. The dashboard note says the update applies globally and to all languages, and that the rollout may take up to two weeks.

Google’s own wording, reported by Search Engine Roundtable, calls it a normal spam update for all languages and locations. Google said it would update the ranking release history when the rollout is finished. On 29 September that completion note was not up yet.

This is the fourth confirmed spam update of 2026, after March, June, and August. Search Engine Journal compared the windows: March finished in about 19.5 hours, June in about two days, and August in two days and 16 hours (18–21 August). September is the first spam update this year that Google described as taking up to two weeks. A full window would run to about 8 October 2026.

A core update reassesses quality and relevance across the index. This release is the spam system. If a headline calls September 2026 a core update, check the dashboard before you rebuild the site around that label.

What the SERP shows

The surface that moves is the organic blue-link list. A URL can leave page 1 on one query and hold on the query next to it. Because Google stages the rollout, the same search can look different on Friday and again the following week. A screenshot from day two is a progress photo, not a final grade.

Search Engine Roundtable’s weekend read put the first heavy wave on 25–27 September, with Saturday the hottest day across several rank trackers. That article was written about five days into a two-week window, and it said more movement was still coming. Treat 25–27 September as the opening stage.

What a live results page can look like during this window:

  • A page that ranked 4 on Monday sits at 18 by the weekend, then shifts again the next week.
  • A URL drops out of a top-100 tracker and is still indexed. Position 101 is invisible to most tools and is not the same as deindexing.
  • One directory or city template falls, while the homepage and the pages with real detail stay.
  • The snippet, the sitelinks, and an AI Overview can remain on the query while the blue link under them changes owner.

Rank trackers are a weak witness this month. Google tightened automated access to results in mid-September, which I covered in Google is blocking scrapers and tracking tools. A blank chart in Ahrefs, Semrush, or Sistrix can be a scrape gap. Open Google, search the query, and compare Search Console before you call it a demotion.

Two different windows

Log these as separate events. Mixing them produces a false “the update started on the 22nd” story.

What stayed on the results page

Google did not announce a new results module with this release. Featured snippets, People Also Ask, AI Overviews, the map pack, and ads were not given a new layout in the spam-update note. If a snippet disappeared, that can be a side effect of the URL under it losing the query. It is not evidence of a snippet redesign. The overlap between snippets and answer engines is a separate topic, covered in featured snippets and AEO.

Google also left these points unpublished:

  • A dedicated link-spam action. Search Engine Roundtable’s read of the release is that this is not a link-spam update. A disavow file is not the first response.
  • A named site-reputation-abuse action. Rented host pages remain a standing policy risk, which I explain in parasite SEO, but Google did not say September only targets that pattern.
  • AI-written content as the sole target.
  • The share of queries affected.
  • A change inside one named spam system.

Discover is a different surface. Forum posts during the weekend described Discover and ad-revenue drops. Those are operator reports, not a measured industry figure and not a confirmed Discover update. Check the Discover performance report on its own if that channel is where the loss shows up.

Which pages move

Google pointed sites back to the existing spam policies. Pages that already conflict with those policies are the ones this system is built to push down or out of results. The patterns I still see in audits:

  • Scaled or templated URLs with little that is unique to that page
  • Doorway sets that swap a city or a keyword into the same copy
  • Thin affiliate pages that repeat the merchant’s text
  • Mass publishing, human or AI-assisted, with no review and no first-hand detail
  • Cloaking, sneaky redirects, or hidden text
  • Expired domains rebuilt only to pass authority
  • Third-party commercial pages on a news or education host with little editorial control

A site with a real editorial process can still move a few positions. Any confirmed rollout shakes the top 10. A catastrophic loss clustered on the thin templates is the pattern that matches a spam system. The August 2026 spam update used the same policy list and finished in about three days. September uses a longer clock, so the SERP can keep sorting into the first week of October.

How to check your own SERP

Do this before you rewrite anything. Five days of a fourteen-day rollout is not a verdict.

  1. Pick ten money queries. Use the phrases that actually send customers, not a 500-keyword export.
  2. Search logged out, or in a private window, and note the location Google is using. Write down the ranking URL, the position, and whether a snippet or AI Overview is on the page.
  3. Open Search Console for 24 September onward and compare clicks and impressions with the prior two weeks. Split the chart at 24 September so the earlier unconfirmed swing stays visible as its own period.
  4. Inspect the URL. If URL Inspection says the page is indexed, a missing tracker row is not deindexing.
  5. Compare one live search with the tool. If Google shows the page and the tool does not, trust the live SERP. The scraper block is still in effect.
  6. Check again after Google marks the rollout complete on the ranking releases page. Positions can move once more before that date.

If a URL dropped

If Search Console and a live search agree that rankings fell inside the 24 September window, work the spam policies. The sequence is the same one I use after August, with a longer wait before you call the result final:

  1. Confirm the timing against 24 September, not against every dip in the month.
  2. Walk templates, affiliates, programmatic URLs, and third-party content against Google’s spam policies.
  3. Merge, noindex, or rewrite pages that do not answer the query on their own.
  4. Put a real author, sources, and something original on the pages you keep.
  5. Fix accidental noindex tags, wrong canonicals, and redirect chains. A technical error can look like an algorithm hit.
  6. Request indexing on the URLs you changed, then give the spam systems time. Google has said recovery from automated spam systems can take months of sustained compliance.
There is no reconsideration form for a normal algorithmic spam demotion. Manual actions are a separate report in Search Console. If that report is empty, the fix is the site itself, recrawled over time.

For how Google finds and ranks a page in the first place, start with what SEO is. If the loss is only inside a rank tracker, start with the scraper block before you touch the content.

September 2026 SERP update FAQ

Is the September 2026 Google update a core update?

Google labeled it a spam update. It enforces existing spam policies. It is a different system from a broad core update. The March and May 2026 core updates were separate releases earlier in the year.

When did the September 2026 spam update start, and is it finished?

It started on 24 September 2026 at 09:15 PDT. Google said the rollout may take up to two weeks, which points to about 8 October if the full window is used. It was still rolling out on 29 September.

What changed in the SERP?

Organic rankings can move in stages across the two-week window. The first widely reported wave was the weekend of 25–27 September. Google did not announce a new results feature, a new AI Overview layout, or a featured-snippet redesign.

Was the volatility on 22–23 September this update?

Search Engine Roundtable recorded that swing before the announcement, and its launch notes say the pre-announcement movement was not this spam update. Keep that period separate from the rollout that began at 09:15 PDT on 24 September.

Did Google target AI content?

Google did not name AI-written content as the target. Scaled pages with nothing original remain exposed whether a person or a model drafted them.

How is this different from the August 2026 spam update?

August ran from 18 to 21 August and was done in about three days. September uses the same kind of spam-policy enforcement on a stated window of up to two weeks, so the SERP can keep moving into early October. Recovery advice is the same: fix policy problems and wait for recrawl.
