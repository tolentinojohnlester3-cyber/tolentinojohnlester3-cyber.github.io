45+ AI tools for SEO and GEO

AI tools for SEO and GEO fall into ten jobs: keywords, technical crawls, content briefs, on-page plugins, rank tracking, link outreach, local listings, AI-search monitoring, GEO visibility, and GEO writing. You do not need 45 seats. Pick one tool per job, keep Search Console as the source of truth, then add a GEO tracker when a client asks who ChatGPT cites.

Map tools to the job

The market now sells “AI SEO” on every pricing page. Most of those products still do one of ten old jobs, with a chatbot bolted on. Group them by the work, then ignore duplicate logos. Semrush, Ahrefs, and Surfer show up in several slices because they are suites — not because you need three other suites next to them.

This pairs with the visibility stack in layers of SEO: classic SEO still crawls and ranks; AEO wins snippets; GEO wins citations inside chat tools.

SEO tools vs GEO tools

SEO tools measure Google: keywords, crawls, backlinks, map pack, blue-link positions. GEO tools measure generative engines: whether ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Copilot name your brand, and which URL they quote. Overviews sit in the middle — they are still a Google SERP feature, so AEO writing plus a GEO tracker both apply.

Classic SEO jobs

1. Keyword research

Find demand, SERP type, and parent topics before you write. Common options: Semrush, Ahrefs, KWFinder, Ubersuggest, KeySearch. I start in Semrush or Ahrefs, then confirm the query in Google Search Console after the page is live. Free-tier keyword tools are fine for a first pass; they are not a second source of truth.

2. Technical SEO

Crawl JavaScript, status codes, canonicals, and index bloat. Screaming Frog, Sitebulb, Lumar, SEObility, and the site-audit modules inside Ahrefs all do this. Frog or Sitebulb on a laptop is enough for most WordPress sites I ship. Lumar (formerly Deepcrawl) is for very large inventories.

3. Content optimization

Turn a keyword into a brief, then score the draft against competitors. Surfer SEO, Clearscope, Frase, MarketMuse, and Dashword live here. Surfer is the one on my daily stack. Treat the score as coverage, not a ranking guarantee — Google’s spam systems still punish scaled filler, AI or not. See the August 2026 spam update.

4. On-page SEO

Titles, robots, schema, and sitemaps on the CMS. On WordPress that is usually Rank Math or Yoast SEO. Rank Math is my default when I need FAQ, HowTo, and Article JSON-LD without a second plugin. The markup still has to match visible copy — see types of structured data.

5. Rank tracking

Daily or weekly positions by keyword, device, and location. SE Ranking, AccuRanker, Nightwatch, plus Semrush and Ahrefs position tracking. I use SE Ranking so rank data is not locked inside the same suite I already use for links. AccuRanker is the faster specialist if that is all you buy.

6. Link building

Prospect, pitch, and watch referring domains. Ahrefs and Semrush for graphs; BuzzStream, Pitchbox, and Respona for outreach CRM; SurgeGraph and similar writers for assisted drafts. Outreach tools do not replace a reason to link. If the profile is spam, that is a disavow problem, not a new CRM.

7. Local SEO

Map pack, GBP, and geo-grid ranks. BrightLocal, Local Falcon, Moz Local, GeoGrids, and AIOSEO’s local modules. BrightLocal or Local Falcon when a client lives or dies on “near me.” Pair that with the Google Business Profile guide.

GEO and AI-search jobs

8. AI search monitoring

Watch how your brand appears inside AI products over time. Names in this slice include AthenaHQ, Semrush One / Semrush AI visibility, and Bear AI. Use these when leadership asks “are we in ChatGPT?” every month and you need a dashboard instead of screenshots.

9. AI visibility / GEO tracking

Prompt-level share of voice against competitors. Peec AI, Profound, Rankscale, Morningscore, Gumshoe, Sight AI, Rankability, plus GEO views inside Nightwatch and Surfer. This is the new rank tracker. Run a fixed prompt set weekly — branded, category, and “best X in [market]” — the same way you would freeze a keyword list.

10. GEO content optimization

Write so models can extract a clean answer and attach it to your entity. Surfer, Frase, Rankability, Contently, and citation helpers such as scite overlap here. The tactic is still the GEO playbook: original numbers, a named author, FAQ extracts, and llms.txt. A GEO writer that only restates the SERP will not get cited.

Profound, Peec, and Rankability are not replacements for GSC. They answer a different question. Keep both if the client sells in AI-assisted research journeys.

The lean stack I actually run

On most of the 67 sites I have handled, the paid stack is small. Everything else is a specialist add-on.

  1. Semrush — keywords, site audit, competitive overlap, and now some AI visibility.
  2. Ahrefs — link profile and content gap. I do not run two full suites for the same report.
  3. SE Ranking — independent rank tracking.
  4. Surfer SEO — briefs and on-page content scores.
  5. Google Search Console + GA4 — free clicks, queries, and conversions.
  6. Rank Math — on-page and schema on WordPress.

Add Screaming Frog for JS-heavy crawls, BrightLocal for map grids, Pitchbox or Respona when outreach volume is high, and Peec or Profound when a client needs ChatGPT citation share in the monthly deck.

If a vendor cannot tell you which of the ten jobs it owns, it is a feature inside a tool you already pay for. Do not stack three “all-in-one AI SEO” platforms.

AI tools for SEO and GEO FAQ

What are the best AI tools for SEO and GEO in 2026?

There is no single best suite. Pair a classic SEO platform (Semrush or Ahrefs), a crawler (Screaming Frog), a content scorer (Surfer or Frase), Search Console, and one GEO tracker (Peec, Profound, or Rankability) if you need AI-answer reporting.

Do I need 45 SEO tools?

No. Most logos in those roundups overlap. One tool per job is enough. Extra seats usually duplicate keyword data you already have.

What is the difference between SEO tools and GEO tools?

SEO tools measure Google rankings, crawls, and links. GEO tools measure whether generative engines cite your pages. You still need the SEO layer; GEO is extra reporting and extractable content.

Which tools does John Lester Tolentino use?

Daily: Semrush, Ahrefs, SE Ranking, Surfer SEO, Google Search Console, GA4, and Rank Math. Add-ons: Screaming Frog, BrightLocal, outreach CRMs, and a GEO tracker when the client needs AI-search proof.

Is Surfer SEO a GEO tool or an SEO tool?

Both. Surfer started as on-page content optimization for Google. It now also sits in GEO writing and some AI-visibility views. Use it as a brief and score tool first.
