How to optimize your site using GEO strategies

GEO is generative engine optimization — the work of earning citations inside ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, and similar tools. Ranking in Google still matters. GEO is the extra layer that makes AI systems name your brand, quote your numbers, and link your URL when they answer.

What GEO is (and is not)

People now ask models the same questions they used to type into Google. The model writes an answer from retrieved pages, training data, and tools. If your page is clear, unique, and easy to attribute, you show up as a source. If your page is a rewrite of ten other posts, the model has no reason to pick you.

GEO is not stuffing “ChatGPT” into titles, paying for fake reviews of your brand inside a chatbot, or replacing classic SEO. Models still prefer pages that already rank, load, and look like a real expert wrote them. GEO sits on that base — it is layer 3 in the layers of SEO stack (SEO → AEO → GEO → AIO → SXO).

Working definition: GEO is the practice of making your site the source an AI system can quote with confidence — original facts, a stable entity (who you are), and machine-readable markup so the citation is accurate.

GEO vs SEO vs AEO

Same website, three jobs. Mix them up and you optimize for the wrong surface.

AEO and GEO overlap. A 50-word definition under an H2 helps Google’s overview and a chatbot that needs a clean extract. GEO then adds what snippets do not require: numbers nobody else has, a person the model can attach to the quote, and third-party pages that already mention you.

Six GEO strategies that earn citations

1. Publish citeable original data

Models prefer a number they can attribute. “SEO matters” is generic. “In 3 months this site earned 92.7K clicks at 22.4% CTR” is a fact a model can quote. Use:

  • First-party metrics (Search Console, analytics, CRM) with a date range
  • Small original studies, pricing tables, or process counts from your own work
  • Named quotes from a real person with a job title and city

Put the stat in a short paragraph near the top, repeat it in a table or FAQ, and label the source (“Google Search Console, last 3 months”). Do not invent figures. A wrong stat that spreads in AI answers damages the entity you are trying to build.

2. Lock the entity: one name, one site, one story

Generative engines resolve “who is this?” before they trust a page. Inconsistent names split that graph. Pick canonical facts and reuse them everywhere:

  • Full name, job title, city, official URL, email
  • Same wording on the homepage, About, author box, LinkedIn, and schema
  • Same Organization / Person JSON-LD @id on every page

This portfolio uses one Person entity, an llms.txt citation block, and identical NAP on contact. That is GEO hygiene: when someone asks an AI “Who is John Lester Tolentino?”, there is one answer to retrieve.

3. Write answer-first pages

Chat answers are short. Help the model extract without guessing:

  1. Turn the query into an H2 (“What is GEO in marketing?”).
  2. Answer in 40–60 words in the first paragraph under it.
  3. Expand with steps, a table, and examples.
  4. Close with an FAQ that repeats the same facts in Q&A form.

Avoid burying the definition after a long intro. Avoid keyword stuffing. Models discard padding; they keep the sentence that defines the term.

4. Add a machine-readable layer

This is where GEO meets AIO. Make the page parseable:

  • JSON-LD — Article / BlogPosting, FAQPage, HowTo, Person, Organization, BreadcrumbList. See types of structured data.
  • llms.txt at the site root — a plain-text map of who you are, canonical facts, and key URLs.
  • robots.txt — allow GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, and Applebot-Extended if you want inclusion. Block them only as a policy choice.
  • Descriptive headings, tables, and alt text — models read HTML structure more reliably than screenshots of text.

5. Earn mentions off your own domain

Chat tools often retrieve a mix of your site and third-party pages. If Wikipedia, a news site, LinkedIn, a directory, or a partner article already states the same facts, the model can corroborate you. That is not “buy a thousand guest posts.” It is:

  • A complete LinkedIn profile that matches the site
  • Citations on industry pages you actually earned
  • Consistent local listings if you are a local business (see Google Business Profile optimization)

Conflicting bios (“SEO manager” on LinkedIn, “CEO” on the site) make the model hedge or skip you.

6. Keep pages fresh

Retrieval systems overweight recently updated documents. Add a visible dateModified, refresh stats, and expand sections that already get impressions. A 2023 guide with no update date loses to a thinner 2026 page that names current tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot).

How to measure GEO

Treat GEO like a lab, not a vanity dashboard:

  1. Build a prompt set — 10 questions customers actually ask (“Who is [brand]?”, “Best way to [your service] in [city]?”, a comparison you want to win).
  2. Run them weekly in ChatGPT (with search/browse on), Gemini, and Perplexity. Screenshot citations.
  3. Score each run — cited with URL / mentioned without URL / absent / cited a competitor.
  4. Watch analytics — Perplexity and ChatGPT referrers, branded search lift, assisted conversions after a citation week.

Google Search Console will not report “GEO clicks.” Some AI answers send no visit at all. That is still a win if the quote is accurate and the brand is spelled right — awareness you used to buy with PR.

GEO launch checklist

Use this on any money page you want models to quote:

  • One canonical definition in the first 100 words
  • At least one original, dated statistic or process count
  • Named author with photo, title, and location
  • Person + Article + FAQ JSON-LD that matches visible text
  • llms.txt lists this URL and the same bio facts
  • AI crawlers allowed in robots.txt (unless you opted out)
  • Internal links to related guides so the topic cluster is obvious
  • Updated date after every material change
  • Prompt-set test logged after publish

Speed and indexation still gate GEO. If Google cannot fetch the page, neither can most retrieval pipelines. Fix sitemaps and crawl errors first — sitemap “Couldn’t fetch” and load speed are not optional extras.

Do not write content “for the AI” that a human would bounce from. Models are trained on what people find useful. Thin, repetitive, or unattributed pages fail GEO for the same reason they fail SEO.

FAQ

What does GEO mean in SEO?

GEO stands for generative engine optimization. It is the work of earning mentions and citations inside AI chat tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Copilot — not a replacement for ranking in Google.

Is GEO the same as AEO?

No. AEO targets answer surfaces on the search results page (snippets, People Also Ask, AI Overviews). GEO targets standalone generative tools. The same answer-first writing helps both.

How long until ChatGPT cites my site?

There is no SLA. Pages with unique data, a clear entity, and existing search visibility get retrieved sooner. Plan in weeks for prompt tests, not overnight rankings. Keep the source crawlable and updated.

Do I need llms.txt for GEO?

It is not a ranking factor in Google. It is a convenient, official summary for LLM crawlers and for people who want a single source of truth. Pair it with schema and a real article — a txt file alone will not get you cited.

Should I block GPTBot?

Only if you do not want your content in OpenAI training or retrieval. Blocking GPTBot can reduce ChatGPT visibility. Allowing it is a GEO choice; it is not required for Google rankings.

Can GEO replace backlinks?

No. Off-site mentions still help models corroborate you, and classic links still help SEO. GEO adds another distribution channel. It does not cancel E-E-A-T or technical SEO.
