37 places AI checks before recommending your business

ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Copilot do not recommend a business from a homepage. They corroborate the entity — name, city, category, hours, reviews — across maps, directories, social profiles, Wikipedia, Reddit, and news. If those sources are empty or disagree, you do not get named.

Why AI looks past your website

A recommendation is a trust decision. Classic SEO still gets you crawled and ranked. AEO still wins the snippet. GEO is what happens when someone asks a model “best [category] near me” or “who should I hire for [job]?” The model does not pick the page with the densest H2s. It looks for an entity that shows up in more than one place, with the same facts.

That is why a polished site with a thin Google Business Profile, zero reviews, and no third-party mentions often loses to a messier competitor who is all over Maps, Yelp, and a local Facebook group. Retrieval plus training data plus tool use (maps, browsing, review APIs) all pull from the same public graph.

Working rule: AI recommends the business it can verify. Your website is the claim. The other 36 sources are the witnesses. One empty witness is fine. Ten silent witnesses, or two that contradict your NAP, and the model skips you.

This is layer 3 in the layers of SEO stack. You still need a crawlable site. You then need off-site proof a generative engine can quote without guessing.

The 37 sources in eight clusters

Do not open 37 tabs and create dead profiles. Group the list. Own the cluster that matches how people already research your category. A restaurant lives or dies on maps and reviews. An agency lives on Clutch, LinkedIn, and case-study mentions. A SaaS product lives on G2, Capterra, and Product Hunt.

1. Owned web

Your website is still source one. It must state the legal name, city, category, and a 40–60 word answer to “who is this / what do you do.” Add Organization or LocalBusiness JSON-LD, an author, and llms.txt so the claim is extractable. See types of structured data. A slow or unindexed site cannot be recommended — fix that before directories. Pair this with the GEO playbook.

2. Maps and the local graph

Google Business Profile, Google Reviews, and Google Maps are the default local knowledge graph. Hours, categories, photos, and review replies are what Gemini and Google AI Overviews already sit on. Apple Maps and Bing Places feed Apple Intelligence and Copilot. Same NAP everywhere. If you hide the address as a service-area business, say so consistently. Full setup is in the Google Business Profile guide.

3. Social profiles and transcripts

Facebook page, Instagram, LinkedIn company page, and YouTube prove the brand is staffed and current. Models ingest captions and, more importantly, YouTube transcripts — a 12-minute explainer is a citeable document. The original list also repeats Facebook: treat the Page as the official profile, and treat groups, comments, and community threads as a separate signal. A locked, empty, or name-mismatched profile is worse than no profile.

4. Consumer review sites

Yelp, Trustpilot, TripAdvisor, Better Business Bureau, Angi, and Thumbtack are category-specific. A clinic does not need TripAdvisor. A hotel does. A home-service brand needs Angi or Thumbtack more than G2. Ask for reviews you earned. Fake volume is a spam problem, not a GEO tactic — see the August 2026 spam update.

5. B2B reviews and the company graph

G2, Capterra, and Clutch are what ChatGPT cites for software and agencies. Crunchbase locks founding year, HQ, and website. Glassdoor and Indeed tell the model you have employees, not a one-page brochure. Claim the listing even if you have three reviews. An unclaimed Capterra page with a competitor’s description is a poisoned entity.

6. Knowledge graph

Wikipedia and Wikidata are the cleanest machine IDs a model can attach to a brand. Do not write your own Wikipedia article if you are not notable. You can create a Wikidata item when the entity is real and sourced, then reuse that Q-ID in schema. This is AIO hygiene: one name, one official URL, one identifier.

7. Community Q&A

Reddit threads, Quora answers, Stack Exchange, industry forums, Nextdoor, and Product Hunt are where models go when the brand site sounds like marketing. You cannot spam these. You can answer the thread that already exists, ship a launch people actually comment on, and leave a paper trail of useful posts under a real name. A single honest Reddit thread often outweighs ten press releases.

8. Media and spoken word

News articles, trade publications, “best in your city” listicles, your own podcast transcripts, and podcasts you guested on are earned corroboration. Transcripts matter more than the audio file. If a journalist misspells the company name, get it corrected — that misspelling becomes a split entity.

What to do first

A 10-source stack that agrees beats 37 abandoned logins. I run this order on client sites.

  1. Lock the entity on the site — one name, one city, one canonical URL, matching schema.
  2. Claim Google Business Profile and reply to every review. This is the highest-leverage local source.
  3. Match Apple Maps and Bing Places so Copilot and Apple Intelligence are not looking at a ghost listing.
  4. Claim LinkedIn plus one social channel you will actually post on. Empty Instagram does not help.
  5. Pick one review network your buyers already use — Yelp, Trustpilot, G2, Clutch, Angi — and earn real reviews there.
  6. Publish one transcript (YouTube or podcast) that answers the recommendation query in spoken language.
  7. Only then chase listicles, Wikidata, and community threads.
Track citations with one GEO tool if a client needs a monthly chart. That is a reporting job, not a 37-profile job. Map that in AI tools for SEO and GEO.

How to audit whether AI will recommend you

Do this weekly until the answers stabilize, then monthly.

  1. Prompt ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity with “best [category] in [city]” and “who is [brand]?” Use a logged-out or fresh thread.
  2. Compare who gets named, linked, or skipped. Screenshot the answer.
  3. Trace. Ask “what sources did you use?” Then search site:reddit.com [brand], Maps, GBP, and the review network for that category.
  4. Fix the one source that is empty or contradictory — wrong phone, old address, competitor occupying the Capterra page — then re-test in two weeks. Models lag.

If every prompt names a competitor with worse content but more reviews, the gap is not another blog post. It is cluster 2 or cluster 4.

37 places AI checks FAQ

Does ChatGPT really visit all 37 sites before recommending a business?

No. The 37 are the public places a model is likely to have seen in training, browsing, maps tools, or retrieval. You cannot control the crawl. You can make sure those pages exist, agree, and are not empty.

What are the most important places AI checks first?

Your website, Google Business Profile, Google reviews, Google Maps, and one third-party review site that matches the category. LinkedIn and a consistent NAP across Apple Maps and Bing Places come next.

Do I need Wikipedia to get recommended in ChatGPT?

No. Wikipedia helps when you are already notable. A local shop with a complete GBP and real reviews will get named without a Wikipedia page. Fake Wikipedia is a policy violation, not GEO.

Why does AI recommend a competitor with a worse website?

Because maps, reviews, Reddit, and directories corroborate them. GEO is entity proof, not just on-page copy. Fix the silent or contradicting sources.

Should every business be on G2, Yelp, and TripAdvisor?

No. Pick the network buyers in your category already read. A SaaS tool needs G2 or Capterra. A restaurant needs Google, sometimes Yelp or TripAdvisor. Empty profiles in the wrong network waste trust.
