Google is blocking scrapers and tracking tools

Ahrefs, Semrush, and Sistrix are having a harder time reading Google results. That is a data-access problem, not proof your pages lost rankings. Here is what changed in September 2026 and how I diagnose visibility now.

By John Lester Tolentino, Senior SEO Specialist
September 21, 2026 Sta. Rosa, Laguna

Key takeaway

Google blocking scrapers is a data-access problem, not a ranking update. Trust Search Console and a live search first. Ahrefs, Semrush, or Sistrix going blank is not proof you fell off page 1.

What happened

In mid-September 2026, Google tightened the ways automated systems read Search. Rank trackers, SERP APIs, and AI scrapers that used to parse a results page and copy every destination URL are now hitting blocks, rate limits, and wrapped links.

Search Engine Roundtable reported the shift around 13 September 2026. Nozzle said most scrape attempts were blocked and that usable data fell by about 80%. Sistrix posted that collection was running at a reduced rate after Google changed how results are delivered.

Google’s public line is familiar: it deploys technical measures against evolving abuse. It did not name Ahrefs, Semrush, or Sistrix. It also did not post this as a core update or a spam update on the Search Status Dashboard.

If a dashboard says a URL “vanished” this week, confirm it in Google Search Console and in a live search before you rewrite the site. Tool charts can lag while Google is winning the scrape fight.

This is not a ranking update

A ranking update changes which pages Google prefers. A scrape block changes whether a third-party tool can see those pages. Those are different problems with the same symptom: a scary chart.

September already had unconfirmed volatility around 4 September, a partial reversal around 13 September, and this data-collection squeeze in the same window. Stack those events and a tracker can show a cliff that never happened in clicks. For crawl, index, and rank basics, see what SEO is.

The last confirmed ranking incident before this scrape story was the August 2026 spam update (18–21 August). Do not merge that policy refresh with a September data-access problem.

Why google.com/goto matters

For years, a results page exposed the destination URL in the markup. A tracker could read position 1 through 100 without opening each site. Google has been wrapping more organic links in a google.com/goto/... passthrough. The visible snippet still looks normal. The href is an encoded Google URL. A server-side redirect then sends a real click to the page.

That breaks cheap parsing. Tools cannot decode the wrapper locally. HEAD requests are often refused. Following every redirect for a multi-page rank check can take hundreds of requests. Rate limits become the bottleneck, not storage.

This sits on top of an older cost shock: Google removed the num=100 parameter in 2025, so one request no longer returns a hundred results. Trackers already paid more to see the same SERP. The goto wrapper raises that bill again.

What Google is protecting

  • Bulk copying of Search for AI training and answer products
  • Industrial rank tracking that hammers results like a bot army
  • Abuse that hides behind “research” user agents

Human search still works. Your browser is not the target. The robot that used to vacuum SERP data, rankings, and outbound link lists is the target.

How the big three tools reacted

Vendors will adapt. They always do. In this window they did not adapt at the same speed, which is why one login can look fine and another looks like a disaster.

ToolWhat teams reported
AhrefsPicked up recoveries fastest in several public comparisons
SemrushShowed the same pattern later, with a reporting delay
SistrixSaid collection ran at a reduced rate while processes were rebuilt

I still use these products for research and competitive context. I do not let any of them be the only evidence that a client “fell off page 1.” For how I map tools to jobs instead of buying every seat, see AI tools for SEO and GEO.

What to trust instead

First-party Google data does not depend on scraping Search from the outside.

  1. Google Search Console. Clicks, impressions, queries, and landing pages. Export the last 28 days and the prior 28 days. Annotate 13–16 September separately.
  2. URL Inspection. Confirm “URL is on Google” before you treat a tracker blank as deindexing.
  3. A live search. Open an incognito window. Search the query. Note the real URL, sitelinks, and whether an AI Overview ate the click.
  4. Analytics. Organic sessions and conversions by landing page. A rank chart without a session drop is a measurement story.
  5. Server logs or CDN logs. If Googlebot is still hitting the URL, crawl is not the failure.

If Search Console itself cannot fetch a sitemap, that is a different technical issue. I documented the usual fixes in sitemap “Couldn’t fetch”.

Trust order for September 2026: Search Console and live Google first. Analytics second. Rank trackers third, and only when two tools agree.

A cleaner SEO workflow

A tougher Search ecosystem does not kill SEO. It kills lazy reporting. I changed three habits on client work this month.

1. Stop treating position as the KPI

Position is a sample from a tool that may be half-blind. Clicks, qualified sessions, and conversions still come from Google’s own pipes. If those hold, the business is not “down” because Sistrix is late.

2. Diagnose missing URLs with coverage, not screenshots

A page can be live, indexed, and useful, and still fail to appear in a tracker that cannot resolve goto links. Coverage reports and query filters tell you whether Google kept the URL.

3. Keep the site worth ranking

Google blocking scrapers does not relax quality rules. Unique titles, a clear page job, and a crawlable hierarchy still decide which URL Google keeps as the main result. That sits in the same stack I cover in layers of SEO — crawl, answer, citation, then conversion.

Expect vendors to restore most tracking. Expect it to stay more expensive and less complete than 2024. Build reports that still make sense if the scrape pipe narrows again next quarter.

Scrapers and tracking tools FAQ

Did Google launch a September 2026 core update?

No confirmed core or spam ranking update was on the Search Status Dashboard for this scrape story. The latest confirmed ranking incident before it was the August 2026 spam update.

Does this mean my site was penalized?

No. Blocking scrapers affects tools that copy results. It does not, by itself, demote your pages. Use Search Console clicks and URL Inspection before you assume a penalty.

Should I cancel Ahrefs or Semrush?

No. Keep them for keywords, links, and content gaps. Do not use one delayed rank chart as the only slide in a client report this month.

Why do three tools show three different rankings?

They are adapting at different speeds. One vendor may resolve goto redirects. Another may still be sampling fewer queries. Disagreement is a collection problem until first-party data agrees with them.

Can I still track rankings at all?

Yes, with a smaller keyword set, first-party Search Console queries, and occasional live checks. Daily top-100 grids for thousands of terms will stay noisier and costlier.
